Pigments

The old Gond colours, by their own names: geru is the red ochre dug out of a riverbank, haldi is turmeric, patta is crushed leaf, chuna is limestone or rice paste, soot is wood-fire soot, and neel — indigo — was always rare.

Infills

The signature of the whole tradition: every flat field is combed with a repeated micro-motif. Dot rows were Jangarh Singh Shyam's own and came out of Gond tattooing; the water droplets are Durga Bai Vyam's; the young shoots are the kind that come up in a flooded paddy field. An established artist's infill identifies them the way a signature does.

The speakers

Gond has no portraiture and no interest in likeness, so these are assemblies of flat patterned parts told apart by attribute — what they carry, what animal stands with them, what grows out of them. They breathe, they blink on a clock, and they lean into a new line. Click one to give it a line to lean into.

The murals

One for each beat of the legend, cut into the young banyan that rises when a root is freed. Limed ground, a border of crescents the way a digna floor is edged before it is filled, figures on one line, read across.
